Generated by `node scripts/backtest/backtest.mts`. 100 synthetic buyers, fixed random seed, so anyone can rerun it and get the same numbers. No AI and no PayPal calls.

## What this shows
- Healthy cafés: the scorecard approves 25 of 25, 25 of them at the full $5,000 limit.
- Risk the numbers show (late invoices, disputes, a failed repayment or an oversized order): it stops 16 of 25. The 9 it still approves have one bad number among good ones, and 9 of those get only the smaller $2,500 limit. A plain points table is lenient when only one thing is wrong, which is a known trade-off of simple scorecards.
- Risk only the documents show: it approves 25 of 25, 25 at the full limit. The warning signs are in a reference letter, the shipping address or the order mix, which the scorecard does not read. **That is the gap the AI analyst covers**, and why the AI may only tighten a decision, never loosen it.
- New cafés with a thin file: the scorecard approves 18 of 25, all at the smaller limit at most (0 at the full limit). The other 7 are not approved, and the AI cannot override that.

## Honest limits
- The buyers are synthetic and the "truth" labels are set by how each group is built. Group C is built so the risk sits outside the scorecard's inputs, so this measures the size of the gap, not the AI.
- How the AI does on real cases from the demo is in `docs/AI_EVAL.md` (repeated live runs on the hosted demo).
